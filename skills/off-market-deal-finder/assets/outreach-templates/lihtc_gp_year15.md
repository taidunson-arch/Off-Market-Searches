# Template: lihtc_gp_year15

Archetype: `lihtc_partnership_forprofit_gp` approaching Year 15 (compliance end) or Year 30 (extended-use end). Decision maker: the GP's managing member (Oregon LP filings list every general partner, ORS 70.610; corroborate with the LURA and the trust-deed signature block) plus the syndicator's asset manager as a courtesy contact.

Gates before use: `preservation_law` (Oregon PuSH notices and 30-day qualified-purchaser match apply to any PSA on a covered property); `lihtc_tenant_protections` (3-year decontrol after extended use ends); `dnc_scrub` for calls. Acknowledge the §42(i)(7) ROFR and qualified-contract mechanics explicitly; never contact tenants; never characterize OHCS's or HUD's position.

## Letter to the GP managing member

```
[Date]

[GP managing member name], Managing Member
[GP entity name], General Partner of [Owner LP name]
[Principal office address]

Re: [Property name], [situs address] ([units] units; placed in service [year])

Dear [Mr./Ms. Last name],

[Company] acquires and recapitalizes affordable apartment communities in [state], and we work with general partners at the end of the compliance period and at the end of the extended-use period.

By our reading of public records, [property name] reaches the end of its [compliance period / extended-use period] around [year]. Owners at that point are usually weighing three paths: a buyout of the investor limited partner and continued ownership, a resyndication with new credits, or a sale. We can be a counterparty on any of the three: funding the LP exit, partnering on a resyndication, or acquiring the property and keeping it affordable under a new regulatory agreement.

We understand that a right of first refusal or purchase option may exist in the partnership agreement, and that Oregon's preservation statute gives public purchasers notice and matching rights. Our approach respects both; we would rather be the counterparty you choose inside that framework than one who ignores it.

If a conversation would be useful, I can be reached at [business phone] or [email]. If the partnership's plans are set, please let me know and I will not follow up.

Sincerely,
[Name]
[Company]
```

## Courtesy call to the syndicator asset manager (same week)

Introduce yourself, state that you have written to the GP, and ask whether the fund has a disposition timetable for its Year-15 assets in the state. Do not negotiate with the LP for the GP's asset; the purpose is to avoid surprising either party and to learn whether LP interests have moved to an aggregator.

## Year 30 variant (LIHTC_EXTENDED_USE_END <= 36 months)

Replace the three-paths paragraph: "As the extended-use agreement approaches its end, owners typically choose between a preservation sale or recapitalization that extends affordability and a conversion over the statutory decontrol period. We acquire on the preservation side, and where the property is subject to Oregon's publicly supported housing notice requirements we are prepared to work alongside OHCS and the city as a qualified purchaser or their designee."

## HAP variant (HAP_EXPIRATION <= 24 months on the same asset)

Add: "The Section 8 contract renewal decision in [year] is something we would approach as part of the same conversation, including Mark-Up-to-Market where rents support it."

## Do not

- Name the LP's capital-account position, the ROFR price formula, or any tenant.
- Suggest a qualified-contract request as a tactic or imply what OHCS will do with one.
- Send anything to the registered agent or the property management office as the decision maker.
