# Template: housing_authority_partnership

Archetypes: `housing_authority`, `government` (PHA, city, county, Metro, urban-renewal agency). Route `partnership_preservation`; tier cap B; acquisition score shown for context only. Decision maker: development director or real estate director from the agency website. These owners rarely sell; the realistic roles are RAD or Section 18 conversion partner, mixed-finance co-developer, or disposition partner when the agency has already decided to dispose.

Gates before use: `preservation_law` where the asset is covered; public-agency procurement rules (expect an RFQ/RFP rather than a negotiated sale).

## Introduction (email to the development director)

```
Subject: Partnership interest — [Property name] and your repositioning pipeline

[Name], [Title]
[Housing Authority / Agency]

[Company] develops and recapitalizes affordable housing in [state] and has worked with public housing authorities on [RAD conversions / mixed-finance redevelopment / preservation acquisitions]. We follow your board materials and understand [property name] is approaching [a Year 15 decision / a RAD conversion / the end of a regulatory agreement] around [year].

If the authority is considering a development partner, a tax-credit co-general partner, or a disposition under HUD approval, we would like to be on your list for any solicitation and to offer a short briefing on how we have structured similar transactions. We can also share preservation-capital options where the authority intends to keep ownership.

[Name] | [Company] | [Business phone] | [Email]
```

## Notes

- Watch the authority's board agendas and HUD disposition approvals rather than recorder documents; those are the real signals.
- Expect a formal procurement; the value of this outreach is being known before the RFQ issues.
- If `buyer_profile = qualified_purchaser` and the authority is itself a qualified purchaser under the state preservation law, the conversation is about coordinating on third-party properties, not about theirs.

## Do not

- Expect or propose a conventional sale.
- Lobby individual commissioners or board members.
- Reference operating distress, REAC scores or audit findings in writing.
