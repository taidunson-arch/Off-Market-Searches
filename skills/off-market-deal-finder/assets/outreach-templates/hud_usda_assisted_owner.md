# Template: hud_usda_assisted_owner

Overlay archetype: any owner type whose property carries a HAP/PRAC/PAC contract, a HUD-insured or HUD-held loan (221(d)(3), 236, 202/811, 223(f)), a Mark-to-Market restructure, or a USDA 514/515 loan. Decision maker: the owner organization and management agent named in the HUD Multifamily Assistance database or the USDA borrower record; the HUD, HFA or RD account executive is a process contact, not a decision maker. Combine with the owner's base template (`single_asset_llc`, `lihtc_gp_year15`, `nonprofit_partnership`, `institutional`).

Gates before use: `preservation_law`; `lihtc_tenant_protections` (enhanced vouchers at opt-out; 3-year decontrol; USDA prepayment protections); `assumption_approval` (HUD TPA for any assumption). Never contact tenants; never represent what HUD, OHCS or RD will approve; never suggest opt-out as a tactic.

## Owner letter (append to the base template's first paragraph)

```
[Property name] participates in [the project-based Section 8 program / a HUD-insured loan under Section [SOA] / the USDA Section 515 program], and we have experience with the decisions those programs put in front of owners:

- Section 8 renewal: the choice among renewal options, including Mark-Up-to-Market where contract rents sit below market, versus the opt-out path and its one-year notice.
- HUD-insured debt: the prepayment window and whether to refinance, assume, or sell with the loan in place, subject to HUD approval.
- Section 236: IRP decoupling and prepayment approvals, and what a recapitalization looks like with a new use agreement.
- USDA 515: a transfer with Rental Assistance preserved, or prepayment with its tenant protections.
- Mark-to-Market: treatment of the HUD-held second and third notes in a sale or recapitalization.

We would approach [property name] within those rules and with the HUD [or RD] account executive informed from the start.
```

## Process call (same week as the owner letter)

Call the HUD regional office multifamily account executive (or the OHCS HCA contract administrator, or the RD state office multifamily specialist) to introduce yourself as a prospective transferee, ask about the current contract term and renewal status, the loan's prepayment restrictions, and the standard transfer or TPA process and timeline. Record what you learn as `REPORTED` with the date and contact role; do not represent it as the agency's commitment.

## Notes

- A HAP contract expiring within 24 months under annual renewal is a conversation about renewal economics, not a departure; ask the owner which renewal option they intend.
- A 236 or 221(d)(3) loan at or past maturity ends the use agreement unless decoupled or refinanced; nonprofit owners here get the mission-partnership script.
- If the owner has filed a PuSH notice or a Section 8 opt-out notice, the qualified-purchaser process is already running; say that you intend to work inside it.

## Do not

- Contact tenants, resident councils or service providers.
- Promise HUD, OHCS or RD outcomes.
- Frame opt-out or prepayment as a value-unlock strategy in writing.
